# `queue.py`'s new lint rules are our contract and have no coverage

The Q956 re-vendor brought `CITATION_WINDOW` and `--citation-window`, the `exhibit:` prefix, the citation fragment and line-range checks, the disk-against-index reconciliation, and the open-question route rules.
[maintaining-backlog.md](../development/maintaining-backlog.md) now teaches three of them to row authors, so they are what this repo asks of a row.

`scripts/docs/queue-test.sh` covers none of them.
It went from 102 checks to 102 across that re-vendor, and its one touched case was repaired rather than extended: the resolving-citation fixture grew from a zero-line file to twenty lines because the new line-range check correctly called the old one a citation past the end of the file.

The three documented rules were verified by hand in review of [#1886](https://github.com/actions-gateway/github-actions-gateway/pull/1886) and all three hold: a 5-line drift is quiet at the default window and reported at `--citation-window 0`, a 40-line drift is reported at both, and the `exhibit:` prefix suppresses the check entirely.
A hand check is not a gate, and nothing here would notice a later re-vendor changing any of it.

The contrast that makes this worth a row rather than a shrug is in the same PR: `pr-mergeability-watch.py`'s one new exit got three cases, each shown failing against the guard it names, while `queue.py`'s six new rules got none.
The asymmetry is not a judgement about which mattered more, it is where attention happened to fall.

`queue.py` is the one declared fork ([skills.md](../development/skills.md#vendored-from-the-skills-repo)), which cuts both ways: the rules are upstream's to change, and this repo is the one that has written them down as its own contract.
Cover the three documented rules at least, paired so a clean store passes and one carrying the defect fails, matching what the suite already does for every other checker.
