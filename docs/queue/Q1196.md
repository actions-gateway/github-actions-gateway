# run-parallel reports wall time per suite, so scripts-test CPU is unread

**The capped `scripts-test` job is bound by total work, and the one number it prints per suite cannot rank work.** Ten CI readings, all green and all on `ubuntu-latest`, put the job's elapsed time at 7.0-7.4% of the summed per-suite wall time:

| Reading | Elapsed | Summed suite-seconds | Ratio | Slowest suite |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 runs on `main`, 2026-10-10 to 10-24 | 94-155s | 1,332-2,086 | 0.070-0.074 | `check-registry-mirror-render-test`, 3-5s before the end |
| Q1117's PR, run 1, 2026-10-24 | 158s | 2,259 | 0.070 | `gate-list-test` 118s |
| Q1117's PR, run 2, 2026-10-24 | 110s | 1,564 | 0.070 | `check-roadmap-test` 78s |

Q1117 cut the suite that always finished last from 152s to 63s and 30s by running its cases concurrently, and the ratio did not move.
What moved is the gap: the job now ends 32-40s after its slowest suite instead of 3-5s, which is the queue draining at the runner's aggregate rate.
So no single suite is the floor, and shortening `check-roadmap-test` or `gate-list-test` the same way would buy no job time either.

**The levers left are fewer CPU-seconds or more cores**, and the first needs a reading nobody has.
`run-parallel.sh` reports wall time per label, and under a cap of 16 on 4 vCPUs that time includes every core a suite shared, so it ranks waiting as well as work.
Q1117's suite shows the gap: about 1.2 CPU-seconds per checker run locally, 14 runs, roughly 17 CPU-seconds, against 152s of wall time on CI.
Q1105 measured the whole job at 528-582 CPU-seconds locally on 2026-09-16 at 130 suites, which would make that suite about 3% of the work, but that division crosses two dates and two suite counts and is an estimate, not a measurement.

**The step:** have `run-parallel.sh` record each child's user+sys CPU beside its wall time, in the block it already prints.
Bash's `times` in the child subshell, or the rusage of the reaped process, are the two candidates; which survives the `( pipeline ) &` wrapper and `wait -n -p` is unmeasured.
With that column a CI run names the suites holding the throughput bound, and the case for cutting any one of them becomes a share of the job rather than an inference from wall time.
