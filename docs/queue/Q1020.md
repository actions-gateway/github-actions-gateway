# Measure the mirror cache hit/miss timing gap from inside a Kata guest

Both mirror topologies and the decision between them shipped: [`overlays/shared-tenants/`](../../deploy/registry-mirror/overlays/shared-tenants/kustomization.yaml) renders the shared set, the default renders the isolated one, and [Choosing a mirror topology](../operations/kata-dind-workloads.md#choosing-a-mirror-topology) carries the cost, the risk and the thresholds.
What is left of the original row is the one thing it called unmeasured: whether a cache hit is distinguishable from a miss **from inside a Kata guest**, across the bridge NAT, on this Deployment shape.

**What was measured, and where it falls short.** On 2026-08-28, against `registry:3.1.1` at the pinned digest run locally as a pull-through cache: blob hits at 10 to 70 ms against misses of 400 to 640 ms, and manifest hits and misses overlapping (medians near 360 ms and 430 ms, a warm 445 ms against a cold 407 ms), because a pull-through cache revalidates a manifest upstream on every request.
That was taken from a laptop over the public internet, not from the position an attacker occupies, so it bounds the channel rather than measuring it.

**This no longer decides anything, which is why it is small.** [Q1022](Q1022.md) records a measurement of `GET /v2/_catalog`, which names every cached repository on the same port the worker policy admits, so a shared set exposes the repository list with no timing involved.
A refuted timing channel would not make a shared set private, and a confirmed one adds a second route to information already readable.
Take it to sharpen the guidance and to know what is left once Q1022 closes the catalog, not to settle the topology choice.

**How to take it.** It needs a live Kata worker: a probe in the job on the Kata lane, beside [`egress-negatives.sh`](../../scripts/e2e/egress-negatives.sh), which already runs there for the reason this measurement needs (a plain pod cannot answer what holds at the end of a path leaving a micro-VM guest through a bridge NAT).
Time blob fetches rather than manifest fetches, since the manifest arm has already come back null.
Warm one repository and leave a second cold, interleave the fetches, and report the distributions rather than two numbers: what matters is whether they overlap, not the ratio of the medians.

Attempted and not taken on 2026-08-28: the dogfood cluster was parked at zero nodes across all four pools, `default-pool` and `workers-od` with autoscaling disabled, so taking it meant unparking the cluster by hand rather than riding a window that was already running.
Prefer folding it into the next real Kata e2e window, where it costs a probe rather than a cluster.
