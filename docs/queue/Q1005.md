# `PublishesAvailableJobsFromDelivery` asserts past what its wait covers

**Top of queue per the [flakes-first rule](../development/maintaining-backlog.md#flake-fixes-go-first).**

Measured 2026-08-27 under `make test-integration` over the full `cmd/agc` suite, run 2 of 4 on one unchanged tree (runs 1, 3 and 4 green, so it is load-dependent): `TestListener_PublishesAvailableJobsFromDelivery` failed at `cmd/agc/internal/scalesetlistener/q720_available_jobs_test.go:61`, `expected: 1  actual: 0`, on "capacity of one must not provision the whole queue".

The `require.Eventually` above it had already passed, so the assertion did not fail for want of a delivery.
That wait is satisfied by the availableJobs gauge reaching `queued-1`, and the gauge is published by `setLastStats` on every statistics arrival (`cmd/agc/internal/scalesetlistener/listener.go:703`), which rides on the delivered message.
The provisioner call for the job that message assigned happens after that publication, so `prov.count()` is asserted on a signal the wait does not cover: the gauge says a message arrived, not that its job was provisioned.
On an idle box the gap closes before the next line runs, which is why this holds until the box is busy.

Fix is to wait on the signal being asserted, `prov.count()`, rather than on the gauge that precedes it.
Confirm it can still fail: a capacity of one must keep provisioning to one, so the fix must not weaken the assertion into a bound that a queue-wide provision would also satisfy.

Found while reproducing Q968 under the full suite; unrelated mechanism, filed rather than fixed.
