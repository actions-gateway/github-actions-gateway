# One suite is the whole of the scripts-test fan-out's wall time

**Measured on CI 2026-09-16**, the first capped `scripts-test` job after Q1105: 130 suites at a cap of 16 elapsed 129s against a slowest member of 125s.
The floor is not most of the wall time, it is essentially all of it, and the other 129 suites cost 1,766 suite-seconds between them at a median of 4s.
So the job cannot finish faster than `check-registry-mirror-render-test` whatever happens to the rest, and a further widening or narrowing of the cap cannot reach it ([run-parallel.sh](../../scripts/ci/run-parallel.sh), Q819).

**The suite to shorten is not the one local runs predicted.** On an 18-core M5 Max across four runs at caps of 130, 36, 18 and 9, `check-roadmap-test` cost 77s, 74s, 88s and 83s and was the slowest every time.
On CI it is 103s and second, behind `check-registry-mirror-render-test` at 125s, with `gate-list-test` third at 101s.
Neither moves with the width, so both are intrinsically that long rather than stretched by contention; the local ranking simply does not transfer, which is why this row now names the CI reading.

**What makes the reading possible at all** is the cap.
Uncapped, every suite's wall time inflated toward the step's and the three slowest were only 8% of the total, so no suite was legible as a floor.
Q1105 capped the fan-out and explicitly does not claim to cut the job's wall time; this row is why it cannot, and the two were flagged as near-duplicates for that reason.
They stay separate: a cap bounds how many suites are in flight, and nothing about a cap reaches one suite's own duration.

What these three suites spend their time on is unread, which is the next step.
