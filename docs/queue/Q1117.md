# check-roadmap-test sets a 74s floor under the scripts-test fan-out

**Measured 2026-09-16** while working Q1105, on an 18-core M5 Max across four `make scripts-test` runs at fan-out caps of 130, 36, 18 and 9.
`check-roadmap-test` cost 77s, 74s, 88s and 83s respectively.
It does not move with the width, so it is intrinsically that long rather than stretched by contention, and a fan-out's wall time is its slowest member ([run-parallel.sh](../../scripts/ci/run-parallel.sh), Q819).

**What that means for the job's budget.** At any cap wide enough not to bind, `make scripts-test` cannot finish faster than this one suite, whatever the other 129 cost.
The two runs at caps 130 and 36 both came in at 77s wall against a slowest suite of 77s and 74s, so the floor was the whole of the wall time in both.
Q1105 capped the CI fan-out and explicitly does not claim to cut the job's wall time; this row is why it cannot, and the allocator flagged the two as near-duplicates for that reason.
They are separate: capping bounds how many suites are in flight, and nothing about a cap can reach a single suite's own duration.

**Not measured on CI.** Under contention every suite's wall time inflates toward the step's, which is why the Q1105 reading found the three slowest suites were only 8% of the total there.
The local runs separate them because the cap removes that inflation, so a runner reading needs a capped run to be legible at all, which is what now runs.
The honest next step is to read the per-label block from a post-Q1105 `scripts-test` job and see where this suite lands once the width is bounded.

What it does with the time is unread.
