# `debt` labels 40% of the store, the shape that retired `infra`

**Measured 2026-09-17** with `python3 scripts/docs/queue.py metrics --labels` over 152 rows: `debt` is on **61**, which is 40%.
[maintaining-backlog.md](../development/maintaining-backlog.md#a-label-earns-its-place-by-discriminating) sets the bar at "more than a third of rows" and records that `infra` was retired at 69 of 160, which is 43%.
So `debt` is inside a third of a point of the population share that retired the label it replaced, and it is the only label over the bar: `tests` reads 51, `ci` 44, `docs` 21, `feature` 21.

`debt`'s declared scope is "refactors, dedup, and dead-surface removal with no behavior change", and the question it answers is *what can I clean up without a design decision*.
That question is still a real one, which is what makes this different from `infra`: `infra` had no question of its own and had become "engineering work that isn't docs, tests, or security".
The reading to take is whether 61 rows all answer `debt`'s question, or whether some of them are carrying it because nothing narrower exists.

The same page warns against applying the number past its argument: a store whose work is nearly all one kind runs that label high without anything being wrong, and this backlog is largely internal engineering hygiene by construction.
So the count is the finding and the retirement is not.

**Ask:** whether 40% on `debt` reads as the `infra` failure or as a true reading of what this backlog is.
It is a call about the vocabulary an adopter and every future session navigate the store by, and both answers are defensible on the same number, so nothing here is a measurement waiting to be taken.
If it is the failure, the repair the page prescribes is a narrower split rather than a rename, and every split candidate is a second question about what this repo wants the column to cut.
