# Cap the scripts-test fan-out on CI, which is 128-wide

`unit-test.yml`'s `shellcheck` job carries `timeout-minutes: 5`, set by Q84 (#182, 2026-06-08) when the job ran shellcheck and nothing else.
It has since gained `errexit-prologue-check`, `script-modes-check`, `scripts-test`, `template-library-check`, `tool-pin-check` and `vendored-skills-check`, and the budget never moved.

**Measured 2026-09-13** over the last 18 `unit-test.yml` runs: the job's wall time on runs that *succeeded* is 183-279s against the 300s cap, and `make scripts-test` alone is 121-201s of it.
A successful run at 93% of its budget is not a margin.
Two runs in that same window were cancelled by the cap: `workflow_dispatch` run 34734535917 at 03:03:23, killed mid-`scripts-test` at 5m45s, and `merge_group` run 34738180796 at 04:33:47 for PR #1919, at 323s.

**What the cap costs is a merge-queue eviction, not a red job.** In the #1919 run every step succeeded, including `Complete job` at 04:38:57; the job was marked `cancelled` 35 seconds later, during finalisation.
`unit-test-gate` requires every gating job to have concluded `success` or `skipped`, read `cancelled`, and failed with `a gating job concluded 'cancelled'`; `github-merge-queue[bot]` removed the PR from the queue at 04:40:03.

That is also what makes it expensive to diagnose.
The PR reads `OPEN`, `CLEAN`, every check green afterwards, because those are the `pull_request` run's checks and the merge-group run is not shown on the PR.
The eviction is visible only in the issue timeline and the `merge_group` run list.

**Not caused by whatever is being merged.** The #1919 branch's own `pull_request` run of the same job was 274s and green, and the change under it added ~470ms to `setup-test.sh` locally (2.44s vs 1.97s, two runs each).
The 03:03:23 cancellation predates that branch entirely.
Any PR that touches `scripts/**` can draw this.

**The 201s is contention, not slow tests, and that is the half still open.** `run-parallel.sh` caps its fan-out at `RUN_PARALLEL_JOBS`, which the Makefile sets from `scripts/agent/local-throttle.sh jobs`, which prints *nothing* when `CI` is set, deliberately, so those runs "stay fully parallel".
So all 128 suites start at once on a GitHub-hosted runner.
Measured 2026-09-13: the same 128 suites sum to 892 suite-seconds locally under a cap of 16 (65s wall, 18 cores) and 7,484 on CI (201s wall), an 8.4x inflation of per-suite wall time for identical work.
On CI nearly every suite reports 170-201s, so each one spans most of the step rather than running in it; the three slowest are 8% of the total, so there is no pathological suite to fix.
The runner's core count was not measured, and the reading does not rest on it: 128 exceeds any GitHub-hosted runner's cores.

Capping the CI fan-out is the fix.
[Q822](Q822.md) already records the design choice and one eviction it caused ("the 119-wide burst the cap removes locally is the burst CI still runs"), and [Q966](Q966.md) owns whether the throttle's number is right; this row is what connects them to a wall-clock budget.
The count grows one suite per new gate (119 at Q822, 128 a week later), so a budget sized to today is a budget that expires.

**Shipped so far.** The cap went to 10 minutes (#1921), the stopgap.
Then `scripts-test` was split into its own job, which is what actually clears the eviction: 86s of work left in `shellcheck` against its 5-minute cap and ~253s in the new job against 10.
What remains is the fan-out.

**This row was derived twice.** The same constraint was found hours earlier in the `v1.8.0-rc.1` session, from the dogfood-dispatched run rather than a merge queue (the job ran 5m45s, 03:06:23-03:12:08), and `constraint-first` there reached the same answer: split first, raise the timeout only as a stopgap.
Nothing was filed, so it was re-derived from scratch.
That is the cost this row exists to stop paying a third time.
