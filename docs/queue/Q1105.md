# The scripts-test job's timeout evicts a PR from the merge queue

**Event:** the `scripts-test` or `shellcheck` job of `unit-test.yml` concludes `cancelled` with every step green, or a PR leaves the merge queue because `unit-test-gate` read a gating job as `cancelled`.

Both mitigations have shipped and neither is proven to close the trigger, which is why this parks rather than retires.

**What shipped.** The cap went to 10 minutes (#1921) and `scripts-test` split into its own job, which is what cleared the eviction: 86s of work left in `shellcheck` against its 5-minute cap and ~253s in the new job against 10.
Then the CI fan-out was capped at four times the runner's vCPUs, taking a 4-vCPU runner from 130 concurrent suites to 16.

**What the cap is evidence for.** It cuts per-suite wall-time inflation, and it cuts the job's wall time; the second took more readings to establish and is qualified below.
Measured on CI across this change's two capped jobs, 2026-09-16: 130 suites at a cap of 16 spent 1,766 and 1,394 suite-seconds against 7,484 uncapped three days earlier at 128 suites, so the inflation fell four to five times on the venue itself.
Measured 2026-09-16 on an 18-core M5 Max, CPU-seconds is flat across every fan-out width from 9 to 130 (528-582s), so oversubscription wastes no CPU at that ratio.
That reading says nothing about wall time on a runner: it was taken at 7.2x on 18 cores, and the cost a 4-vCPU runner pays at 32x is scheduler and memory contention, which this box at that ratio cannot see.
No local reading ranks the caps at all: within-cap variance swamps the between-cap difference (cap 18 ran 106s and 182s on one tree, cap 0 ran 78s and 151s) as ambient load climbed 30 to 112 across the sweep.
Re-running that sweep here cannot settle it either, because the swing is the box's own throughput moving under the instrument rather than an effect too small to see; a ranking needs a quiet host or CI's own job history instead of more local runs.
The 4x multiple is the largest oversubscription the repo has evidence is harmless, not a measured optimum.

**The floor the cap cannot reach** is [Q1117](Q1117.md): the fan-out elapsed 129s and 103s against slowest members of 125s and 97s, so one suite is nearly the whole of it and no cap reaches one suite's own duration.

**The cap cuts the job's wall time, and the margin is not structural.** Re-derived 2026-09-16 over every `unit-test.yml` run since 2026-09-14 carrying the job on the `ubuntu-latest` lane: 38 uncapped runs took 137-245s against a 600s cap, median 202.5s, and this change's five capped runs took 120-148s, median 141s.
All five fall below the third-smallest uncapped run and two below the uncapped minimum; an exact one-sided Mann-Whitney gives U=184 of 190, p=3.0e-05.
Ambient conditions do not explain it, which is checkable rather than assumable: uncapped runs starting inside the capped window ran at a median of 204.5s (n=10) against 201s outside it (n=28), so the capped runs were interleaved with normal-speed ones rather than taken in a quiet period.

**What that reading still cannot settle.** It is observational, and treatment is perfectly confounded with branch, because no uncapped run of this job exists on the branch carrying the cap.
A diff audit is the only control available, and it finds no competing change on that branch: `unit-test.yml` is comment-only, `run-parallel.sh` adds one `printf`, and the new verb is one `jobs` never reaches.
The five capped readings are also repeated measures on one tree, so they are not five independent draws and the p-value is optimistic.
That penalty is mild rather than disqualifying: across the uncapped branches 72% of the variance is within-branch rather than between it (pooled within-branch sd 19.6s against 23.2s overall), which puts the effective capped sample near 2.4 rather than 5.
What stays non-independent across the five is branch-specific, so it is the confound above restated rather than a second limitation, and the diff audit is the control for both.
The size and consistency of the gap carry this finding; the p-value does not.
The ranges do overlap at the edge, and a rerun of any capped PR adds a reading, so re-derive both sets rather than trusting these counts.
Two further runs put the job on the scaleset lane and both failed, at 314s and 364s ([Q1116](Q1116.md), a different defect).
The suite count grows one per new gate (119 at Q822, 128 on 2026-09-13, 130 on 2026-09-16), so a budget sized to today expires.

Revive with the per-label block from the failing run: under a capped fan-out the suites no longer all stretch to the step's duration, so the slowest ones are legible and name what to shorten.
