# `expect_rc` reports a signal death as a wrong verdict

`expect_rc` compares the command's status numerically against the wanted one, so a status of 128+n means a killed process rather than a verdict, and it is reported as the gate having answered wrongly.
`run-parallel.sh` already draws that line one level up and says so in its own output: SIGTERM (143) under host contention is not a gate failure, while 137 is the OOM killer and is.
The nested suites do not, so a contention kill inside `scripts-test` reddens `make check` as a failed assertion and reads as a defect in the gate under test.

Measured 2026-08-28 under local `make check` (18 cores, load 22-33, 48 gates fanned out with ~106 script suites nested inside one of them): `check-endpoint-parity-test` reported `FAIL a fake missing the endpoint fails: expected rc=1, got rc=143`.
The captured output on that same failure carries the gate's correct report (`the e2e fake serves no GET /repos/x/x/actions/runs/x`, then `endpointparity: 1 finding(s) across 1 request site(s)`), so the checker had already reached the verdict the assertion wanted and was killed before it could exit 1.
The suite passed four times standalone straight afterwards on the same tree.

The fix is to treat `rc >= 128` as a kill rather than a mismatch: name the signal, and either fail with that distinction stated or hand it back the way `run-parallel.sh` does.

**Key the fix on the compare, not on `expect_rc`.** The name is the obvious handle and it under-reaches: `expect_rc` is copied into eight suites, but the defect is any numeric comparison of a captured status against a wanted one, and that shape appears 51 times across 31 suites under a spread of names (`expect_rc`, `expect_retry` at `scripts/e2e/validate-cluster-test.sh:140`, `want_rc`, `want`, and bare inline compares).
Measured 2026-08-28 over `scripts/**/*-test.sh` for `((rc [!=]= …))` and `[[ "$rc" [!=]= … ]]`.
A pass that greps for the helper's name fixes eight and leaves twenty-three suites able to report the same false finding.
A shared assertion helper the suites source would close it once rather than 51 times, and that is the larger change to weigh.

Distinct from [Q912](Q912.md), whose mechanism is the endpoint-parity fake failing to start inside its bound; here the fake started, served, and was reported on.
Distinct from [Q822](Q822.md), which is about the uncapped fan-out that produces the contention; this row is about what the suites do with a kill once it lands, and is worth having whether or not the fan-out is ever capped.
