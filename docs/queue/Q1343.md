# GMC may mint ServiceAccount token Secrets in tenant namespaces

**A GMC compromised while a namespace is marked may be able to mint a token for any ServiceAccount there, and keep it after the unmark.** Found while bounding the unmark lead closed by Q1328, whose fix stops the GMC writing a RoleBinding that would keep its Secret reads after an unmark.

The GMC holds Secret `create`/`update` cluster-wide, and `gmc-tenant-resource-guard` confines those writes to marked tenant namespaces without looking at the Secret's `type`.
Kubernetes' token controller fills a Secret of type `kubernetes.io/service-account-token` annotated `kubernetes.io/service-account.name: <sa>` with a long-lived token for that ServiceAccount, and the GMC can read the Secret back through its `gmc-tenant-secret-reader` binding.
The GMC writes no Secret of that type, so a guard rule denying it costs nothing in normal operation.

**Unverified.** That the token controller fills a GMC-written Secret on a supported Kubernetes version was not measured: envtest runs no controller manager, and no kind cluster was available in the Q1328 session.
Measure it on kind before writing the rule, and say which ServiceAccounts a tenant namespace carries that hold more than `agc-tenant-role` (a tenant's own ServiceAccounts are the case that matters).

**Done when** the GMC ServiceAccount is denied creating or updating a Secret of type `kubernetes.io/service-account-token`, with an envtest case in `cmd/gmc/internal/controller/integration/tenant_resource_guard_test.go`, or the measurement shows the controller does not fill such a Secret and that is written into `docs/design/05-security.md` §5.1 Residuals.
