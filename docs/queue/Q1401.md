# Recover a fail-fast matrix's siblings behind an aggregate job

On a matrix with fail-fast siblings and an aggregate job (`if: always()`, red when one it needs is), 2.1 leaves the siblings cancelled where the retry before Q1319 re-ran them.
Of 42 sampled runs where nothing independent failed, 30 have that shape, all of them one repository's workflow ([the readings](../plan/q555-automatic-recovery-design.md#readings-for-a-cancelled-sibling-2026-11-24)); no run in the sample lost a runner.

**The fix:** GitHub re-runs a job's `needs:` dependents with it (M6), so the jobs the attempt after a single-job re-run ran, besides the killed one, are its dependents by GitHub's own reading.
A dependent red there is not another failure, so once every `failure` left is one, the release calls `rerun-failed-jobs`.
That takes the cancelled siblings, and any number of killed legs still parked, and never a job that failed on its own without needing the killed one.
It needs `cancelled` not counted as another failure, which Q1319's pull request builds and asks a decision on.

**What it costs:** a second slot, so `maxEvictionRetries` of at least 2, the default; a second attempt; and the parked entry kept after its last killed job while a cancelled job is left.

**What it must know:**

- That the attempt was GAG's call and not a person's, so the entry records the attempt its accepted single-job call started.
  An accepted call names a job ID of the attempt read before it, and GitHub refuses an earlier attempt's, so that attempt is the next one.
- Which re-run job was the killed one, whose new failure is its own: matched by display name, and no re-run at all where two re-run jobs share it.

**What it would misread:** a dependent that fails for its own reason, such as a cleanup job with a bug, is re-run a second time.
The single-job call already re-runs it once, and nothing in the list says why it failed.

"Started after the killed job completed" is not the fact to use: over 195 runs with two or more failed jobs it named 171 jobs, 73 of which needed nothing of the first (the same readings).
Q1399 is separate: it tells a cancelled sibling from a timeout, and leaves an aggregate as another failure.
