# Full-history checkout outlasts the 5-minute timeout on docs jobs

**Top of queue per [flakes-first rule](../development/maintaining-backlog.md#flake-fixes-go-first).**

Twice on 2026-11-03 an `actions/checkout` with `fetch-depth: 0` ran into its job's `timeout-minutes: 5` and the job was cancelled.
The second one evicted PR #2255 from the merge queue at 21:28:39Z: in merge-group run [41720191876](https://github.com/actions-gateway/github-actions-gateway/actions/runs/41720191876), `release-pins` sat in checkout from 21:18:38Z to 21:23:37Z, and `doc-links-gate` failed on the cancelled job while every other merge-group workflow passed.
The first was the `pages.yml` `build` job of run [41687543983](https://github.com/actions-gateway/github-actions-gateway/actions/runs/41687543983) attempt 1, in checkout from 17:47:42Z to 17:52:41Z, and green on rerun.

The stall is inside the fetch.
`release-pins`'s log shows `git fetch --no-tags --prune --no-recurse-submodules origin +refs/heads/*:refs/remotes/origin/* +refs/tags/*:refs/tags/*` start at 21:18:38Z and print nothing until the cancel.
A `release-links` job in the same run ran the identical fetch and needed 2m34s to finish it.
The fetch takes every branch and tag, but the remote holds only 72 branches and 81 tags, so the ref count does not explain the time.

Checkout durations, measured over the last 150 runs each of `doc-links.yml` and `pages.yml` (2026-11-01T22:30Z to 2026-11-03T21:47Z), taking each job's `Run actions/checkout` step:

| Day | Depth | Jobs | Median | p95 | Max | Over 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-02 | full history | 817 | 21 s | 24 s | 59 s | 0 |
| 2026-11-02 | shallow | 1270 | 4 s | 6 s | 24 s | 0 |
| 2026-11-03 | full history | 305 | 22 s | 171 s | 319 s | 66 |
| 2026-11-03 | shallow | 503 | 5 s | 28 s | 64 s | 1 |

So the 5-minute budget is about twelve times a normal day's p95, and the timeouts come from slow windows on GitHub's side: every fetch over 60 s started in the 16:00, 17:00, 18:00 or 21:00Z hour of 2026-11-03, and none on 2026-11-02.
Full history multiplies the slowdown, from a shallow worst case of 64 s to 319 s in the same windows.
What caused the windows is unverified.

Seven full-history jobs run under `timeout-minutes: 5`, and any of them can evict a PR from the queue in a slow window: `em-dash`, `design-scope`, `row-commits`, `release-pins` and `release-notes` in `doc-links.yml`, and `build` and `validate` in `pages.yml`.
`release-links` (10 minutes) took the 319 s maximum on 2026-11-03, which a 5-minute budget would have cancelled, and `render-overflow` and `publish` have 20.
Candidate fixes, none measured yet: fetch less, with `filter: blob:none` on the full-history checkouts, provided every gate still reads what it needs (several `git show` other refs, which fetch blobs on demand); give the checkout its own step timeout and a retry, so a stalled fetch restarts instead of using up the job; or raise the job budget for the full-history jobs alone.
The fix should state which of these it measured against a slow window, not just against a quiet day, since a quiet day passes all three.
Sweep the idiom: `publish.yml`, `validate-candidate.yml`, `status-lint.yml`, `manifest-validate.yml`, `release-freeze-watch.yml` and `dependabot-rebase-stale.yml` also take `fetch-depth: 0`, and any of their jobs with a tight `timeout-minutes` has the same exposure.
