# The out-of-memory class can fire before the pod names the job it ran

GitHub gives a scale-set job to whichever runner asks first, so a worker often runs a job other than the one it was provisioned for; `JobStarted` is what moves the pod's `run-id` and `job-name` annotations to the job it really runs (Q1152), and it is never written to a terminal pod.
The out-of-memory class reads those annotations when the pod turns `Failed`, for the job whose floor it raises and the run it re-runs.

Measured on dogfood on 2026-11-24 (run 45324839276): the runner was killed at 04:16:17, the class fired at 04:16:24, and the job's `JobStarted` was handled at 04:16:35, 30 s after the job started.
In two other runs that day 2 of 16 and 4 of 19 workers ran the job they were provisioned for, so the two usually differ when jobs are assigned together.
There the runner and its job happened to be the same.

What follows is read from source and not reproduced: a runner killed before its `JobStarted` is handled raises the floor, and re-runs the run, of the job it was provisioned for.
The `startedElsewhere` check in `recoverDisruptedScaleSetWorker` withholds where another worker started that job, which loses the recovery of the job that died; where no worker has, the class acts on the wrong one.
The window is a job killed within its first half minute or so, and it applies to every arm that reads an unstamped worker, though the out-of-memory class alone raises a limit by the job's name; the re-run ledger records the name and other arms read it too.

A candidate is to defer the class by a short grace while the pod is unstamped, or to ask GitHub which job the runner held: the jobs API names it by `runner_name`.
First reproduce it, by provisioning two jobs together and killing one at once.
