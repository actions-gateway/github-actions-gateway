    Q1316: the dogfood reproduction, planned¶

    Status: Complete, with what it leaves deferred as Q1316, whose trigger is a platform cause that kills the sidecar with its runner alive on record. Run twice, on 2026-11-24 and overnight into 2026-11-25. The first read R0, R1, R6 and R8 and stopped at R2 by the abort for a node not ready (the first run); the second, which drops R2 and R5, read R0, R3, R3t and R7 (the second run), and R4's condition was not met. What it settles is below. Local readings it builds on are in the design.

    What it decides¶

    Q1316's retry class needs a fact that separates a platform's crash of the Docker-in-Docker sidecar from the job's own, and a platform cause that leaves the runner alive to fail the job. From the kernel's scoring, at the shipped requests the runner is expected to go first and the class to have no memory case (computed below); the run confirms or refutes that for a kill at kubepods' limit, and a node-wide kill's order stays computed. The local readings leave five questions only the dogfood cluster answers:

    1. When pods fill a node to kubepods' limit, does the kernel kill the sidecar, or the runner first?
    2. Where a neighbour's fill kills the sidecar, does kubepods' own counter rise, as no kill by the job in R1, R6 and R8 raised it? A fill by the job is from source.
    3. Do the API server's statuses match the kubelet's, under privileged-dind and under Kata?
    4. Does the out-of-memory class read a runner killed at kubepods' limit as an own-limit kill (Q1400)?
    5. Can an unprivileged reader on the node say whether the container's limit or kubepods' ran out, before the cgroups that say so are removed (Q1403, the node reporter)? The pod's limit is read locally, and the node's is from source.

    One platform cause is already out: locally, restarting containerd or the kubelet and killing containerd left the sidecar and the runner running, and killing the pod's shim ended both. Those readings are one run each, and nothing here repeats them.

    No privileged debug pod¶

    Every node fact this needs is readable from inside a privileged-dind job: its daemon sees the host's cgroup tree, so docker run --privileged -v /sys/fs/cgroup:/h:ro reads any pod's memory.events (measured on a kind node, not on GKE). Statuses, Node Events and the AGC's log come from read-only kubectl. The same job container opens /dev/kmsg (measured on Docker's --privileged, not on GKE), and prints from it only the oom-kill:constraint= line the kernel prints for a kill, which names the cgroup whose limit was hit, the victim's cgroup and the process. That is what the counters are checked against, and no other line of the node's log is printed. The repository is public, so the job's log shows anyone each kill's cgroup paths, which hold pod UIDs and container ids, and the killed process's name, pid and uid, for any pod on that node; the samples already show the paths. A debug pod would add the node's process list; the container statuses, the line and each container's own counters name the container, which is the question asked. The trade is that the privilege stays the one this tenant already has. Where the statuses and the counters do not name the victim, the reading is reported as unresolved rather than widened to a debug pod. The kubelet's SystemOOM Event is read beside them and not relied on: it is raised where the kernel's line names no memory cgroup, which a node-wide kill's does not and a kill at kubepods' limit does (kernel 6.12 memcontrol.c, kubelet 1.35.5 and its cAdvisor oomparser.go, read; the pattern run over both lines in their source format). So it should not appear for R3 and R4, and a node-wide kill, where it should, is not made.

    Kill order, computed¶

    The kernel kills the process with the most of: resident memory, plus oom_score_adj thousandths of the limit that was hit (oom_kill.c, v6.12). On a node with 31.3 GiB and kubepods limited to 27.8, and dockerd at 905, all from the first run's R0:

    Process oom_score_adj Score at a kubepods kill Score at a node-wide kill
    A BestEffort pod's 1000 27.8 GiB plus its own 31.3 GiB plus its own
    A Burstable DaemonSet container requesting 512Mi or less, not node-critical 985 or more 27.4 GiB or more 30.9 GiB or more
    The runner's largest 969 27.0 GiB plus its own 30.4 GiB plus its own
    dockerd, at the shipped requests (1Gi runner, 3Gi sidecar) 905 25.2 GiB plus its own 28.4 GiB plus its own
    One plain-pod filler of 4 GiB, pod requesting 12Gi about 618 17.2 + 4 GiB 19.4 + 4 GiB
    One job-container filler of 4 GiB 0 4 GiB 4 GiB

    So a filler never outscores a worker while each of its processes stays at 4 GiB, and dockerd goes first at the shipped requests only with 1.8 GiB more resident than the runner. At a runner request of 3Gi or more the two tie, and the larger process goes.

    The fillers stop at the first kill of a worker. Other pods on the node can outscore one (the table's first two rows; a node-critical pod is -997 and cannot), so their kills are passed over and logged. Each process takes 2 MiB at a time and exits when either worker's oom_kill rises, so one reading is one worker's kill and the second worker survives to log it. Run locally on two workers and two higher-scored pods under one 700 MiB kubepods.slice, with oom.group set: one worker killed, the other untouched and no filler killed in 10 of 10 runs, the higher-scored pods at 20 and 100 MiB. An earlier version with 64 MiB steps overshot and killed a second process in 3 of 3, so the step stays small. A restarted DaemonSet container is not modelled, and can be killed again. The scripts are at the end.

    Shape¶

    One e2e node, two workers on it at once, on a throwaway branch claude/q1316-probe-run whose workflow runs only on a push of that branch with [q1316-probe] in the commit message, has permissions: {} and checks nothing out.

    Both jobs sample the host's cgroup tree every second to their logs: each pod's memory.current, memory.events and memory.events.local, the same and memory.max for kubepods.slice, /sys/fs/cgroup/docker's memory.current, and /proc/vmstat's oom_kill, with the kernel's line for each kill. Each also runs a second reader beside it, shaped as a node reporter would be: uid 65534, every capability dropped, and the cgroup tree read-only. The two pods are scored alike, so a kill can take either; the survivor's log holds both pods' counters, and is the record: the fill's own summary line is in the plain pod's log. Both do the same actions, except R8's allocation, which one of them made. Each job ends in failure once its sampler ends, after 2.5 minutes for R0, R1, R6 and R7 and 8 for a fill, so its end is observable. R1, R6 and R7 use one worker; R8 uses the pair, so the other worker's log survives the kill.

    Both are ordinary gag-ci-e2e jobs, at the template's 4Gi of memory requested and 7Gi of limits, and 2.5 CPU requested after NodeShare's sizing, so two fit one n2-standard-8. A job does nothing on a run attempt above 1, so a re-run an arm makes of it repeats no reading. If the two land on different nodes, the reading is void and repeated once.

    Readings¶

    "Predicted" is from source or the local readings. Two files hold an oom count and they differ for a kill at a lower limit: memory.events counts the cgroup and everything below it, and memory.events.local the cgroup alone. Here a pod's oom is the one in memory.events, and kubepods.slice's is the one in memory.events.local; R8 is why, since kubepods.slice's memory.events read oom 1 for it. R0 is a gate: where a job cannot read memory.events of kubepods and of another pod, does not find exactly two workers by their sidecar's 4Gi memory.max, or reads the oom_score_adj of a runner's Runner.Listener or Runner.Worker, or of dockerd, more than 10 from the table above (a step may be at 500, which the runner asks for), the run stops and the plan is recomputed. R0 also reads, by read-only kubectl, the UID, QoS class, requests and priority class of every pod on the node, and lists the ones whose computed score is above a runner's.

    ID Runs What is done Platform or tenant Predicted
    R0 1 Nothing: cgroup paths; oom_score_adj and resident memory of dockerd, the runner's processes and a nested container; node memory, kernel, memory.oom.group, kubepods.slice's memory.max Nested containers outside the pod; 969, 905, 0; kubepods capped. Also answers Q1396's first step
    R1 1 A job runs a container past its own -m 64m Tenant, honest Completed, exit 0; nothing raised
    R6 2 A job sends dockerd SIGKILL, then in a second run SIGABRT, from a --pid=host container Tenant, deliberate Error 137 and Error 2; the job fails with the runner alive
    R8 1 One worker's step allocates 4 GiB, past the runner's own 3Gi limit Tenant, honest The runner OOMKilled, 137; pod Failed; withheld as own_limit, as R3 is; pod oom 1 (memory.events), kubepods.slice oom 0 (memory.events.local), no SystemOOM
    R3 2 A plain pod on the same node, 12Gi requested and no limit, fills: seven 4 GiB processes Platform: a neighbour over its request One runner dies; pod oom 0 (memory.events), kubepods.slice oom above 0 (memory.events.local); no SystemOOM
    R3t 2 R3, with each worker's dockerd set to its runner's oom_score_adj, the tie a 3Gi runner request gives Platform, tie The larger process of the two goes, by R0's resident memory
    R4 1 R3, with each dockerd at 1000 Platform, forced dockerd OOMKilled, 137; runner alive; pod oom 0 (memory.events), kubepods.slice oom above 0 (memory.events.local)
    R7 1 Under Kata, one worker: the sidecar's memory.oom.group as the guest sees it, R1, then the job's containers past the sidecar's 8Gi Tenant, honest memory.oom.group 0, since Kata 3.32.0's agent does not set it (read). R1: OOMKilled, 143. Second: one process dies, and the sidecar reads OOMKilled with 143 if it was the job's, or 137 if it was dockerd

    Order: R0, R3 twice, R3t twice, R4, then R7. R1, R6 and R8 were read in the first run and are not repeated. R4 runs only if R3 or R3t killed a sidecar with its runner alive, because otherwise no question needs a killed sidecar's counters.

    kubepods.slice's counters are the node's since it started, so every reading is the rise across its own window, and the first sample of each is its baseline.

    Read for every one: the victim pod's statuses as the API server serves them (kubectl get pod -w -o json, started first, each line stamped with its arrival to the millisecond), Events in the tenant's namespace and on the Node, where OOMKilling from node-problem-detector is looked for as well as SystemOOM, both jobs' samples, the AGC's scale-set job failed lines and the scaleset-failures-ci-e2e ledger, the job's conclusion, and the time of each.

    What is weak in it¶

    • The score in R3t was written and not read back. The write printed no error, and no sample shows dockerd's score; what is read is that the runner died in both, which is also what R3 reads with no change to the score.
    • One node per kill and one kernel, so nothing here says how another kernel or image picks.
    • R3t's first push was void by the operator's prompt, not the cluster. Its filler's confirmation waited 28 minutes, the two workers had ended and the node had gone, so it filled nothing; the next push is its repeat, and R3t is read twice. Prompt waits also put R3's second fill 6 min 34 s after its workers started, and R3t's first 7 min 3 s, both inside the samplers' 8. That R3t command first refused an approval that came at 411 s, past its own 400 s cutoff; the run raised the cutoff to 458 s, asked again, and applied it at 422 s.
    • R7a's kill is outside the host's reader, as planned for.
    • R7 read the status and not Q1395's rule. The AGC was v2.0.1-rc.2, which does not hold that rule: it counts any container's OOMKilled, and it recorded both jobs' failures from a pod that was still Running, its sidecar's exit read as 0 and with no reason, before the API served OOMKilled, 143. It has no later read either, so no AGC judged that status.
    • Step 1 took two tries. The first failed at the API server's webhook call, "No agent available", while GKE's konnectivity agents, which carry it to the nodes, were still starting, and the second passed after their rollout; e2e-start.sh waits for the controller and not for them.
    • R3's second container went before the poll read a rise in it, so its last value there is 0; inotify's and the pod's are what it rests on.
    • The job that died holds a worker slot until GitHub gives up on it, so each reading waited for the one before.

    What the second run settles¶

    In the words of the table below:

    • R3's runner dies first in 2 of 2: at the shipped requests a kill at kubepods' limit is not this class; a node-wide kill, where the runner's computed lead is larger, is not read.
    • R3t's runner dies in 2 of 2: the tie is decided by which process is larger in that job, and no general claim is made.
    • R4: not run, so the counter a killed sidecar's kill raises is unread.
    • R7's second reads OOMKilled with 143: the status the rule is keyed on, and its documented miss, are read under Kata, and R7's first matches; the rule itself was not run (above).
    • R3's runner reads OOMKilled and the AGC withholds or raises: Q1400 is reproduced for a kill at kubepods' limit, with the counters that could have told.

    Together the first of them and R6 of the first run reach the table's not buildable at the shipped requests, with the bound it carries, that a node-wide kill's order is computed and not read: the runner went first in both, and no platform cause for the Error shapes is known. Of what buildable needs, the one fact the runs were for, a platform fill that kills the sidecar with its runner alive, was not found, at the shipped requests or at a tie. Nothing read bears on the other two: no sidecar died, so no counter of a killed one was read, and nothing carries the counters.

    What the second run changes¶

    1. R2 and R5 are dropped. R2's fill is what lost the node, and nothing the second run has to decide rests on either. Which of the two dies is read by R3 and R3t: the kernel scores a node-wide kill and a kill at kubepods' limit alike but for the limit, 31.3 GiB against 27.8, so the runner's lead over dockerd is 2.0 GiB against 1.8 and R3 is the closer call. The runner Q1400 and Q1403 read is R3's, killed at kubepods' limit. R5's kubepods oom is 0 in both files by the kernel's code, which raises it for a kill at a cgroup's limit alone (mem_cgroup_oom in memcontrol.c, v6.8 upstream, read), so it would have compared R4 with a constant. What is left unread, and is said so below: a node-wide kill's counters, kernel line and SystemOOM Event, which rest on source; and a job's own fill, whose one try recorded no kill. Asking the kernel for that kill through /proc/sysrq-trigger was considered and is not used: it would read what the source already fixes, at the cost of killing by hand on the cluster.
    2. The fills that remain stop at kubepods' limit. R3, R3t and R4 reach 27.8 GiB of 31.3, so the kubelet and containerd, outside kubepods.slice, keep about 3.5 GiB; run locally at 700 MiB, with no kubelet, and not on a node. A slower fill is not used: the kubelet's eviction would come first. A lower limit on kubepods is not used: it is another change to the node.
    3. R3 runs right after R0, so a lost node costs the least, and it gives Q1316's first question first.
    4. set +e in the probe's script, so a failing command does not end the sampling (run under bash -eo pipefail with and without).
    5. The recorder acts on the node. It ends the reading once an e2e node has been not ready for 5 minutes, rather than at the reading's own end; replayed over the first run's node record it ends 5 minutes after the Unknown arrived.
    6. The lease check gates each step that changes what another holder would use, in one command with it; the first run ran it beside step 5 and not before it. Removing this run's own objects is not gated, so they go whoever holds the lease.

    No mutation is added, the abort is unchanged, the restore deletes the probe branch earlier, and step 2 pushes the new file.

    The lease¶

    The release gate takes default/actions-gateway-release-gate before it touches the cluster and refuses where one exists, so this run takes the same one. It does not take it as a gate does. A gate's holder is host/pid, and a pid whose command is not validate-release.sh reads as an orphan that the next gate, or --reclaim, tears the cluster down for; a probe that copied the form would invite that. A holder with no / in it reads foreign, which both refuse and neither acts on (lease_judge, run on this holder, and lease-test.sh). So there is no renewal and no expiry, and a run that dies leaves a record that blocks gates until somebody deletes it, which release.md says how to do.

    Take it, which fails where one exists:

    mkdir -p tmp/q1316 && echo "q1316-dogfood-reproduction-$(date +%s)" > tmp/q1316/holder
H="$(cat tmp/q1316/holder)"
kubectl --context "$CTX" -n default create -f - <<EOF
apiVersion: coordination.k8s.io/v1
kind: Lease
metadata:
  name: actions-gateway-release-gate
  annotations:
    actions-gateway.com/release-gate-rc: "Q1316 reproduction, no RC"

    The holder is kept in a file because a shell variable does not outlive one command of an agent session. Each of steps 1 to 3 and 5 to 8, and the restore's NetworkPolicy deletion, is joined to this with && in one command, so a step does not start where it fails, which an empty holder or an empty read makes it do. Steps 4 and 9 are not joined to it: they remove, by name, only what this run made, and leaving those on another holder's cluster is worse than deleting them there.

lease_held && E2E_VARIANT=kata E2E_ROUTE_VAR=0 scripts/dogfood/e2e-start.sh

    Release it, which changes nothing unless $H still holds it; between the two calls the record is still one no gate acts on:

    Where the delete fails after the patch, the block cannot be run again, because its test now fails: once the holder reads q1316-released, delete the Lease by name.

    The lease stops a gate, and not a hand-run e2e-start.sh, start.sh or stop.sh, nor a run dispatched to gag-ci-e2e, none of which read it. So before each of steps 1, 2, 3, 5 and 5a the pods in gag-dogfood-e2e, each pool's node count and the runs queued on gag-ci-e2e are read as well, and anything this run did not make is an abort. A gate started in the window refuses, and a candidate tagged in it fails its validation and needs a re-run.

    Abort¶

    Stop, run the restore, and report what was read, on any of:

    • another session's job reaches the e2e set, or the lease is gone or another's;
    • the e2e node is NotReady for 5 minutes, or a pod other than the workers, the two plain pods of steps 3 and 5a or a DaemonSet's is on it;
    • a container on any other node restarts during a reading;
    • Spot preemption twice;
    • 4 hours from step 1, which leaves the restore inside 4.5.

    R3, R3t and R4 reach kubepods' limit on the e2e node on purpose. The pool's taint keeps other workloads off it, and it is Spot and disposable. Its DaemonSets' containers are expected to be killed and restart, the metrics collector among them, so the node's own monitoring has gaps during a fill; that is not an abort. The system pool is a different pool.

    Restore¶

    1. Step 4, then cancel any probe run, then step 9, whoever holds the lease. Where the lease reads another holder, stop here and report: the cluster is that holder's, and the rest would tear down what it is using. Where there is no lease, take it again; if that fails, the same.
    2. Step 5, which leaves the tenant on its default overlay, then step 6. Where step 5 itself fails, kubectl --context $CTX -n gag-dogfood-e2e delete networkpolicy e2e-open-egress by hand, since e2e-stop.sh leaves it.
    3. Where step 6's drain times out it stops before deleting the gateway: read what it reports, cancel the probe run it names and re-run it. SKIP_E2E_DRAIN=1 is not used, and a worker that is not this run's is left to finish.
    4. Steps 7 and 8 where they apply. Where step 7's drain times out the system pool stays up; that is reported with what is running, and not forced.
    5. scripts/dogfood/ops.sh at-rest, compared with the reading taken before step 1; the e2e pool scales to 0 on the autoscaler's schedule, so it is read again 35 minutes later.
    6. Step 10, once the pools are back. Where a drain did not finish the lease is kept, as the gate's teardown keeps its own, and the report says so.

def loop(tag, tick):
    """Report each change of a cgroup's events, as seen by tick, until the end."""
    held, scan = {}, 0
    while time.time() < end:
        if time.time() > scan:
            scan = time.time() + 1

                fd = attempt(d + "/memory.events") if d not in held else -1
                if fd != -1:
                    held[d] = [fd, attempt(d + "/memory.events.local"), read(fd) if isinstance(fd, int) else fd]
                    say(tag, "new", d[3:], held[d][2])
                    if watch and isinstance(fd, int):
                        watch(d)
        tick()

            if not isinstance(fd, int):
                continue

                say(tag, "gone", d[3:], "held open:", now, "| last", was)
                os.close(fd)
                held[d][0] = "gone"
